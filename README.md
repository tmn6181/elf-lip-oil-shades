## What's actually in it, and how it performs

The core Glow Reviver Lip Oil is built on squalane plus apricot, avocado, jojoba and pomegranate oils. That's the whole reason it behaves like a lip product rather than a gloss: those oils are doing the moisturizing work while the formula stays non-sticky, which is the thing almost everyone mentions first.

Verified ratings across retail listings are strong. e.l.f.'s own review stream, as republished on Sephora's Middle East storefront, shows **4.8 out of 5 across roughly 11,700 reviews** for the core oil. Walmart's Canadian listing sits at 4.8 with over 10,000 ratings. The stick version shows 4.8 across 324 reviews on the same Sephora storefront.

A third-party review that goes deeper than the average star rating, Amanda Vitale's writeup at The Aesthetic Edge (updated December 2025), lists the shine as its standout quality, describing it as glossier than most lip glosses she's tested, with a lightweight, nourishing feel and a large applicator that makes overlining easy. Her cons are more useful than the pros, though:

- Tints are very sheer. Most shades read as nearly clear on the lips.
- It needs frequent reapplication, because oil formulas aren't long-wearing by nature.
- You don't need every shade unless you're collecting. Her suggestion was to buy the clear plus one or two darker shades.

That last point is the honest answer to a lot of "which shade should I get" searches. If you can't see it in person, the difference between Pink Quartz and Crystal Clear on your actual lips is going to be small.

## The plumping version is where opinions split

Glow Reviver Plumping Lip Oil adds menthone glycerin acetal, peppermint, spearmint and eucalyptus oils to the base formula. That's your tingle. Reviews on e.l.f.'s own product pages, aggregated through retail listings, show a genuinely wide spread of reactions:

- One reviewer describes the sensation as cooling and minty rather than burning, and rates the plumping "4 out of 10 on the burning scale."
- Another says it built up over time and got uncomfortable enough that they wished it would stop.
- A third is blunter: "It burns... the shades are great," writing in December 2025.
- Separately, a reviewer gives four stars and one specific complaint, that the scent smells chemical to them.

If you've used lip plumpers before and hated the burn, this will not convert you. If you've found other plumpers too aggressive, several reviewers specifically note this one is milder and doesn't sting. The plump effect is temporary, driven by increased blood flow to the lips, so touch-ups are what maintains it.

There are also two dedicated plumping shades worth knowing about: Talk That Taupe and Heavy Petal, which have been available in the four-piece Plumping Lip Oil Kit rather than individually.

## Which formula to pick

The quick version:

1. **Want maximum shine and zero commitment to color?** Core Glow Reviver Lip Oil. It's the $9 original and still the best value in the line.
2. **Want the same thing with sparkle?** Glimmer. Same base, same $9, different finish.
3. **Want visibly fuller lips and like a minty kick?** Plumping. Read the tingle reviews first.
4. **Want color payoff first and shine second?** The Stick. More pigment, softer gloss, and reviewers note it needs reapplication.
5. **Buying as a gift or want an exclusive shade?** The Quad Goals kit at $32, or the Plumping kit at $27.98.

Worth flagging: paying $32 for four oils works out to $8 each, which is cheaper per unit than buying individually at $9 only if you actually want all four shades. If you only want Jam Session, buy Jam Session.
